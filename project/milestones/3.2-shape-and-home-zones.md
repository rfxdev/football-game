# 3.2 Shape and Home Zones

**Status:** Outline

The formation grid: eleven a side's 5×5 grid, with a 1-2-1 in front of the keeper, moving as one block to follow the ball, with each player returning to their cell when not engaged. This is the change that turns [3.1](3.1-dumb-five-a-side.md)'s chaos into something recognisable as football. One layout only, kept compact: five-a-side is here to test basic AI, not formations. Its three lines show how rows interact from the start; nobody is in a wide column, so the wide-column rules are built and table-tested here but first judged in play at [4.1](4.1-eleven-a-side.md).

**Asking:** Did the blob resolve into something that reads as football? Does play have flow? Are goals happening at a reasonable rate? Does the shape follow the ball without lurching, or trailing, when it moves fast?

**Session shape:** full scenarios, **with a friend if at all possible** — two perspectives catch far more than one, and a second player exposes the things you've stopped noticing

**Notes:**

- **This is the milestone that "blobbing" is the named failure mode *for*.** All outfield players converging on the ball reads as chaos rather than football. It's a shape problem more than an AI-intelligence problem: only two players per team go for the ball, and everyone else runs to their anchor, per [Who Goes for the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#who-goes-for-the-ball) and [Off the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#off-the-ball). If it survives this milestone, the anchors in [Formation and Shape System](../../docs/design/match-engine/formation-and-shape-system.md) are the first place to look, rather than the decision logic
- **The opposite failure mode arrives here too: bumper cars.** Players glued to their cells look as wrong as players chasing the ball, just differently wrong. The reference itself breaks shape only for the two players going for the ball, so judge whether that reads as football — and whether the shape's lag behind the ball reads as players reacting — rather than whether anyone else leaves their cell. A gap here is logged for after the clone plays, per [Player Depth](../ideas/player-depth.md)
- **One grid for every team size.** The pitch is final from [3.0](3.0-pitch-and-camera.md), so five-a-side is a layout on the full grid rather than a smaller grid of its own, and [4.1](4.1-eleven-a-side.md) adds a layout and players, not a grid. Which rows the three lines sit in is a layout choice this playtest settles
- **Five players on a pitch built for eleven.** There is more space than the shape will have at 4.1. Judge shape and goal rate against that, not against a full team
- **The shape is tuned in the editor before it's played.** The [shadow formation](../../docs/design/match-engine/formation-and-shape-system.md#anchors) runs there with no match: drag the ball and watch every anchor. Follow fractions, squash and the nudge can be judged there first, and the playtest is left with what only play shows — whether players reaching those anchors reads as football
- **The possession nudge is built here and judged here.** Play with it at zero and with it on: the team should read as open with the ball and compact without it, and still as one formation
- Goal rate is the most useful single number here, and one of the few subjective questions with an objective proxy — but it isn't sampleable in bulk until the headless harness at [5.5](5.5-clock-halves-and-full-time.md)

**Testing:** anchors as a pure function — layout, ball position and possession nudge in, every player's anchor out — table-tested against the always-true criteria in [Formation and Shape System](../../docs/design/match-engine/formation-and-shape-system.md#acceptance-criteria), with the ball in either box and on both touchlines, and over a layout with filled wide columns as well as the 1-2-1. Shape recovery — park the ball, run on, assert every uninvolved player is back within radius of their cell's anchor for that ball position.

**360 Testing:** now that home zones exist, [3.1](3.1-dumb-five-a-side.md)'s blobbing observation is assertable for the first time — team spread, or mean distance from each player to their cell's anchor, as a numeric *floor* rather than a look-and-judge call.
