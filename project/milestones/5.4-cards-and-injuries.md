# 5.4 Cards and Injuries

**Status:** Outline

Yellows, reds, second-yellow sending-off, and in-match injuries — all rolled off the foul detection from [2.3 Tackling](2.3-tackling.md), and all independent of the restart in [5.3](5.3-free-kicks-and-penalties.md).

**Notes:**

- **Three independent rolls off one event, and the reference is specific about all three.** Whether a foul draws a card at all is gated separately; where the foul happened inverts the red/yellow odds when there is no covering defender; injuries roll on every tackle collision regardless of whether it was ruled a foul. Collapsing any two of these into one check will look right in most matches and be wrong in the ones that matter
- Each team draws from a shared budget of injuries and dismissals; once it's spent, neither can happen again that match. Easy to miss, and it changes the shape of a match's back end
- **Injured players play on, carrying the malus below.** Substitutions don't exist until [6.2 Team Management](6.2-team-management.md), so there is no way to take them off before then
- **An already-injured player is about twice as likely to be injured again**, and their severity roll shifts hard toward the severe end — the mildest outcome drops from about 66% to about 22%. Playing on hurt is a real risk, not just a slower player

**Attributes:** Speed, through the injury malus — the first time a match event changes a player's ratings mid-game. An injury subtracts a fixed amount from the speed value, scaled to severity: nothing when uninjured, down to −288 at the worst of seven levels. Against a speed range of 928–1250, the worst injury costs nearly the whole gap between the slowest player and the fastest. The reference applies the malus only to human-controlled teams and skips fully CPU sides — whether to clone that asymmetry is decided here.

**Sprites:** cards, and an injured player.
