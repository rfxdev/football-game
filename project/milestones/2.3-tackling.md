# 2.3 Tackling

**Status:** Outline

1v1 against an outfield opponent: slide tackles, passive duels, and foul **detection**. The act of tackling, not the choice — the opponent never decides to slide; when the AI tackles is [3.3 Decision Loop](3.3-decision-loop.md)'s. The foul stops play; the free kick that follows is [5.3 Free Kicks and Penalties](5.3-free-kicks-and-penalties.md).

**Notes:**

- **Two ways to lose the ball, and only one of them involves the button.** A defender can win it passively just by running into a carrier, resolved on the difference of two `(Tackling + Ball Control) ÷ 2` averages. The passive duel is easy to overlook because it has no input attached, and it is doing a lot of the work
- **The foul rule turns on whether the ball was touched first, not on the angle.** A slide that hits the player before touching the ball is a foul from any angle; one that wins the ball and carries on into the player is checked on direction match, a foul only from behind. A trailing chase rarely wins the ball cleanly, so it lands in that check — worth understanding before tuning it, because the lever is not where it looks
- **The first opposing outfield player, so the first time two outfield kits share the screen.** Nothing new is built, but it is the first real test of the [Kits](../../docs/design/match-engine/kits.md) criterion that a player's side reads at a glance — a kit legible on its own can blur against another mid-tackle
- Cards and injuries roll off this detection but are staged in [5.4 Cards and Injuries](5.4-cards-and-injuries.md)

**Attributes:** Tackling and Ball Control together decide the passive duel — each player's average of the two, compared — so a player strong in one and weak in the other plays the same as a balanced one. Tackling's other effect, recovery after a slide, arrives earlier with the slide itself in [1.3](1.3-contextual-action.md).

**Sprites:** a player brought down by a foul.
