# 2.3 Tackling

**Status:** Outline

1v1 against an outfield opponent: slide tackles, passive duels, and foul **detection**. The foul stops play; the free kick that follows is [5.3 Free Kicks and Penalties](5.3-free-kicks-and-penalties.md).

**Reference:** *Sliding*, *Fouls, Cards & Injuries* in [Match Mechanics](../../sources/swos/match-mechanics.md)

**Notes:**

- **Two ways to lose the ball, and only one of them involves the button.** A defender can win it passively just by running into a carrier, resolved on the difference of two `(Tackling + Ball Control) ÷ 2` averages. The passive duel is easy to overlook because it has no input attached, and it is doing a lot of the work
- **Track whether anything felt *broken* or *cheap*.** "Cheap" is the more valuable signal and the easier one to lose — an AI that dispossesses you with a lunge you couldn't have countered isn't a bug, but it will make the game unfun long before any bug does
- **The foul rule is geometric, not a penalty on the angle.** A tackle that connects with the ball is clean regardless of approach; a tackle that misses and hits the player is checked on direction match. A trailing chase rarely produces a clean connect, so it lands in the foul branch by geometry rather than by rule — worth understanding before tuning it, because the lever is not where it looks
- Cards and injuries roll off this detection but are staged in [5.4 Cards and Injuries](5.4-cards-and-injuries.md)

**Attributes:** Tackling and Ball Control together decide the passive duel — each player's average of the two, compared — so a player strong in one and weak in the other plays the same as a balanced one. Tackling also sets how long a player lies down after any tackle attempt: 30 frames at Tackling 0 down to 9 at Tackling 7. A CPU-initiated tackle ignores Tackling and recovers in a flat 3 frames; the reference runs human and AI recovery under different rules.

**Sprites:** a player brought down by a foul.
