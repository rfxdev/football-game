# 3.0 Pitch and Camera

**Status:** Outline

A real pitch — dimensions, markings, both goals — and a camera that follows the ball across it. The first time the world is bigger than the frame. No change to how anyone moves or kicks: this milestone reframes Phase 1 and 2's football rather than altering it.

**Reference:** *Pitch and View* in [Match Mechanics](../../sources/swos/match-mechanics.md). The pitch is **672 × 848** reference pixels and the view is **320 × 200**, so at [1.1](1.1-movement-and-possession.md)'s scale of 4 the pitch is **2688 × 3392** world units inside a 1280×800 frame. Those are matchable numbers, so most of this milestone exits on a check rather than on taste — the exception is camera *behaviour*, which the disassembly does not fix and which falls back to a playtest judgement.

**Notes:**

- **Pitch and camera are one milestone because they share one exit question: can you read the game at this scale?** Neither half answers it alone — a pitch larger than the frame can't be judged without a camera, and a camera with nothing to follow has no failure mode. Split, they would be two milestones that can't individually exit
- **About 11% of the pitch is on screen, and it scrolls on both axes.** The pitch is 2.1× wider and 4.2× taller than the view, so this is not a camera that only tracks up and down the park. Vertical travel dominates on a portrait pitch, but lateral scroll is real and needs tuning in its own right rather than falling out of the vertical case
- **The camera translates. It does not zoom.** Every feel value tuned in Phases 1 and 2 — run speed, friction, kick power — is calibrated against the world scale set at [1.1 Movement and Possession](1.1-movement-and-possession.md). A camera that only ever moves preserves all of it; a camera that changes zoom silently invalidates it and the retuning bill arrives later, disguised as "the ball feels different now"
- **This is where the aspect-ratio rule gets built.** [0.2 Sandbox Scene](0.2-sandbox-scene.md) deliberately took a fixed frame and accepted bars, because nothing in it was player-facing. Here it is. [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md) settles what the rule *is* — 800 units tall always, width floating 1280 to 1422, bars outside the 16:10–16:9 band — and this milestone is where it stops being a project setting and becomes something to look at. Note that `expand` does not clamp on its own; the width cap and the bars beyond it are code
- **Judge the 16:9 end of the band by eye, because 11% more width is small enough to be fine and large enough to check.** A 16:9 player sees 355 reference pixels of width against the Deck's 320. There is no fairness question — [couch co-op](../../docs/design/game-vision-and-design-goals.md) puts both players on one screen — so the only thing to establish is that the pitch still reads at the wide end and that nothing framed against the 1280 width falls apart at 1422
- **Settle the 848-versus-880 discrepancy before building, not during.** The pitch header says 848 tall; the engine's own bounds check says 880. Best guess is playing surface versus tiled area including the margin behind the goals, but it is unconfirmed, and picking the wrong one puts the goal line 32 pixels out — which [5.2 Out of Play](5.2-out-of-play.md) then inherits as a rule that is subtly wrong everywhere
- **It lands before [3.1 Dumb Five a Side](3.1-dumb-five-a-side.md) because 3.1 is judged by eye from a distance.** Whether the blob reads as a blob is a question about what is on screen. Judging it through a frame that may be cropping the action makes the judgement worth less
- **Not [4.1](4.1-eleven-a-side.md), even though a full pitch is what forces the issue.** Arriving there would mean debugging scroll behaviour and eleven-a-side at once — two novel suspects for every problem, which is the thing the sequence exists to avoid

**Attributes:** Finishing's power multiplier now fires inside a box on a real pitch rather than [2.1](2.1-keeper-drill.md)'s arbitrary one. The rule is unchanged; only the geometry it reads against is.

**Sprites:** the pitch surface and markings. The second goal and its box are [2.1](2.1-keeper-drill.md)'s goalmouth and box repositioned, not redrawn.

**360 Testing:** [0.2](0.2-sandbox-scene.md)'s boundary predicate was written against a frame, and a boundary now means the pitch edge instead. The predicate is unchanged but its geometry no longer comes from the viewport, so [0.3](0.3-ball-physics-test-suite.md)'s corner cases need re-pointing at pitch coordinates — and the goal predicate with them, now that both goals exist in fixed positions rather than one configurable line segment.
