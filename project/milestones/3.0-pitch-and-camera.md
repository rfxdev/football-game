# 3.0 Pitch and Camera

**Status:** Outline

A real pitch — dimensions, markings, both goals — and a camera that follows the ball across it. The first time the world is bigger than the frame. No change to how anyone moves or kicks: this milestone reframes Phase 1 and 2's football rather than altering it.

**Notes:**

- **Most of this exits on a check, not on taste.** [Pitch and Environment](../../docs/design/match-engine/pitch-and-environment.md) fixes the pitch — a 510 × 641 playing area and every line on it — at the reference's numbers, and [Camera](../../docs/design/match-engine/camera.md) the view — 426 × 266 on the Deck — and where it stops. Camera *behaviour* isn't fixed by the disassembly, and falls back to a playtest judgement
- **Pitch and camera are one milestone because they share one exit question: can you read the game at this scale?** Neither half answers it alone — a pitch larger than the frame can't be judged without a camera, and a camera with nothing to follow has no failure mode. Split, they would be two milestones that can't individually exit
- **About a fifth of the pitch is on screen, and vertical scroll dominates.** On the Deck the view covers 63% of the pitch's width and 31% of its length. Lateral scroll is much smaller than the reference's: in open play its camera stops at x 63 and 609, leaving the Deck about 120 world units of sideways travel, 16:9 at 1080p about 65, and 720p none, against the Amiga's 210. Judge whether that little still reads as a camera following play, or just as jitter
- **Camera limits are the view stops in [Camera](../../docs/design/match-engine/camera.md), not the reference's clamp numbers.** The stops are positions on the pitch and hold on every display; the clamps assume the reference's screen size
- **The camera translates. It does not zoom.** Every feel value tuned in Phases 1 and 2 — run speed, friction, kick power — is judged at the Deck's scale set at [1.1 Movement and Possession](1.1-movement-and-possession.md), and the scale only ever changes with the display, never in play. A camera that only ever moves preserves all of it; a camera that changes zoom silently invalidates it and the retuning bill arrives later, disguised as "the ball feels different now"
- **This is where the aspect-ratio rule gets built.** [0.2 Sandbox Scene](0.2-sandbox-scene.md) deliberately took a fixed frame and accepted bars, because nothing in it was player-facing. Here [Camera](../../docs/design/match-engine/camera.md)'s band — width following the display from 16:10 to 16:9, bars beyond — stops being a project setting and becomes something to look at. The width cap and the bars are code, since `expand` doesn't clamp, per [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md). Check that integer scale mode with `expand` fills leftover space with more pitch rather than bars
- **Judge the wide end of the band by eye, at 1080p and 720p.** [Camera](../../docs/design/match-engine/camera.md) settles fairness; what's left is whether the pitch still reads at the widest view — 720p, half as wide again as the Deck — and whether anything framed against the Deck's width falls apart
- **Take the goal lines from their constants, not from the size of anything drawn**, per [Pitch and Environment](../../docs/design/match-engine/pitch-and-environment.md). [5.2 Out of Play](5.2-out-of-play.md) inherits them, so a line placed from a map size is a rule subtly wrong everywhere
- **It lands before [3.1 Dumb Five a Side](3.1-dumb-five-a-side.md) because 3.1 is judged by eye from a distance.** Whether the blob reads as a blob is a question about what is on screen. Judging it through a frame that may be cropping the action makes the judgement worth less
- **Not [4.1](4.1-eleven-a-side.md), even though eleven a side is what needs a full pitch.** Arriving there would mean debugging scroll behaviour and eleven-a-side at once — two novel suspects for every problem, which is the thing the sequence exists to avoid. So this pitch is final: five-a-side plays on it, and 4.1 adds players, not pitch

**Attributes:** Finishing's power multiplier now fires inside a box on a real pitch rather than [2.1](2.1-keeper-drill.md)'s arbitrary one. The rule is unchanged; only the geometry it reads against is.

**Sprites:** the pitch surface and markings. The second goal and its box are [2.1](2.1-keeper-drill.md)'s goalmouth and box repositioned, not redrawn.

**360 Testing:** [0.2](0.2-sandbox-scene.md)'s boundary predicate was written against a frame, and a boundary now means the pitch edge instead. The predicate is unchanged but its geometry no longer comes from the viewport, so [0.3](0.3-ball-physics-test-suite.md)'s corner cases need re-pointing at pitch coordinates — and the goal predicate with them, now that both goals exist in fixed positions rather than one configurable line segment.
