# 1.1 Movement and Possession

**Status:** Outline

A single controllable player who can run, turn, and pick the ball up, plus what carrying it costs. No opposition of any kind.

**Notes:**

- **World scale gets decided here, and the reference has already half-decided it.** SWOS's speeds are quoted in pixels per tick — a Speed-7 player tops out at 2.44, the slide lunge starts at 3.5 and covers about 34 pixels, per [Match Mechanics](../../sources/swos/match-mechanics.md). Cloning those numbers means adopting the reference's pixel as the world unit, so the only open choice is how many screen pixels one world unit gets. Everything downstream follows from that single integer: sprite size, pitch dimensions, and how much football fits on a screen
- **The scale is 4, and the reference picked it.** SWOS runs at 320×200 in mode 13h (`swos.asm:7276`, the 64000-byte framebuffer clear at `swos.asm:14562`, `WIDTH`/`HEIGHT` in `tools/swospp/main/swos.h:13-14`), and 1280×800 is exactly four times that at the same 16:10 aspect. Four world pixels to one reference pixel is pixel-perfect, needs no resampling of sprites drawn for a fixed grid, and inherits the reference's field of view rather than approximating it. Anything non-integer buys a permanently slightly-wrong look that no amount of tuning fixes
- **Pixel-perfect in world space, not necessarily on the glass.** The 4:1 mapping is internal and always exact, so sprites drawn on the reference grid never resample as the game runs. The final scale from viewport to window is a separate step, and it only lands on a whole number at 1280×800 and exact multiples — a 1080p monitor scales the finished frame by 1.35. That is a presentation concern for [3.0 Pitch and Camera](3.0-pitch-and-camera.md), not a reason to pick a different world scale here
- **Record it, because everything downstream is calibrated against it.** Every feel value tuned in Phases 1 and 2 assumes this number, and [3.0 Pitch and Camera](3.0-pitch-and-camera.md) frames that scale rather than redefining it
- **Sprite dimensions and the foot anchor get fixed alongside the scale, and neither is cheap to revise.** The scale says how big a reference pixel is; still open is how many of them a player is tall, and which point on the body is the player's position. Y-sort orders by that point, and whether the ball sits at the feet at reach distance depends on it, so moving it later moves every depth and contact judgement made against it. The reference's proportions are the default, since its reach and lunge distances were tuned against bodies that size
- **This is the last point the control scheme can be judged in isolation.** From Phase 2 onward there is always an opponent to blame for a bad-feeling moment. Spend the time here; a control problem shipped past this point gets mistaken for an AI problem for weeks
- **Turning is the thing to abuse.** Turning with the ball should cost more than turning without it, and whether that difference reads as *skill* or as *sluggishness* is the whole question. See [Player Controller and Input](../../docs/design/match-engine/player-controller-and-input.md)
- **Tune the stick deadzone here, on the pad.** This is the first milestone with anything to position finely. It's a feel value per [Player Controller and Input](../../docs/design/match-engine/player-controller-and-input.md), tuned against real hardware per [Gamepad Input and Steam Deck Parity](../../ways-of-working/gamepad-input-and-steam-deck-parity.md)
- **The contested half of ball control is deferred to Phase 2, but the mechanic is the same one.** Overrunning the ball-control turn threshold strips the ball; here there is simply nobody to strip it to. Building it now means [2.3 Tackling](2.3-tackling.md) adds a defender rather than a mechanic
- **Live stat tuning belongs here.** Being able to change a player's ratings mid-session is what makes the reference's attribute tables checkable rather than theoretical — the speed table alone is a 35% swing top to bottom, and you cannot judge that by reading it

**Attributes:** Speed sets top running speed from a table — 928 at Speed 0 up to 1250 at Speed 7 — and carrying the ball cuts it to 87.5% for everyone. Ball Control sets how far the ball runs ahead of the carrier's feet, and how long a turn can be held before the ball is lost.

**Sprites:** the outfield player standing and running in every facing — the first sprite with a body, drawn to the dimensions and anchor above.

**Testing:** input maps to an intent — direction and action — independent of which device produced it; possession pick-up fires exactly when the reach predicate holds and not otherwise.
