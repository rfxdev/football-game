# 1.1 Movement and Possession

**Status:** Ready

A single controllable player who can run, turn, and pick the ball up, plus what carrying it costs. No opposition of any kind.

**Asking:** when a turn leaves the ball behind, does it read as *your* mistake — a turn made while the ball was out of reach — or as the game's? Two ways to fail: at Ball Control 0 losses feel random rather than mistimed; at 7 the ball feels glued, so timing never matters. This is also where the creep rates in [Carrying the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#carrying-the-ball) are judged, rather than measured before the build, per its Open Questions.

**Session shape:** about 20 minutes, solo, on the pad. Free dribbling in the sandbox at one middle Speed, in planned blocks at Ball Control 0, then 7, then 3. Recorded and logged, per [Session Habits](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md#session-habits).

**Notes:**

- **Feel is judged at the Deck's scale from here.** The world unit is the reference's pixel, per [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md), and the Deck draws it at a scale of 3, per [Sprites](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md). The scale doesn't change a single speed, but it changes how fast things look: at 3 a player takes a third longer to cross the screen than at 4. Every feel value tuned in Phases 1 and 2 is judged at it, and [3.0 Pitch and Camera](3.0-pitch-and-camera.md) frames it rather than redefining it
- **Sprite dimensions and the foot anchor get fixed here, and neither is cheap to revise.** Y-sort orders by the anchor, and whether the ball sits at the feet at reach distance depends on it, so moving it later moves every depth and contact judgement made against it. Both are the reference's, in [Movement](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#movement), since its reach and lunge distances were tuned against bodies that size
- **This is the last point the control scheme can be judged in isolation.** From Phase 2 onward there is always an opponent to blame for a bad-feeling moment. Spend the time here; a control problem shipped past this point gets mistaken for an AI problem for weeks
- **Turning is the thing to abuse.** Off the ball a turn is free; on it, turning while the ball has crept out of reach leaves it behind. Whether a lost ball reads as *your mistake* — bad timing — or as *the game's* is the whole question, and it should read differently at Ball Control 0 and 7. See [On-the-Ball Mechanics](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#carrying-the-ball)
- **Tune the stick deadzone here, on the pad.** This is the first milestone with anything to position finely. It's a feel value per [On-the-Ball Mechanics](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#input-as-intent)
- **The contested half of ball control is built here but judged in Phase 2.** Too many direction changes loses touch only with an opponent near the ball, so here the count climbs and costs nothing. What counts as a change — the 2-tick hold — is judged here, by watching the count; what losing touch costs is [2.3 Contests](2.3-contests.md)'s. Building it now means 2.3 adds a defender rather than a mechanic
- **Live stat tuning belongs here.** Being able to change a player's ratings mid-session is what makes the reference's attribute tables checkable rather than theoretical — the speed table alone is a 35% swing top to bottom, and you cannot judge that by reading it
- **Draw the reach radii and the count.** The pick-up's 5.7 and the pressure rule's 8.5 are the first reaches in the game, and the direction-change count is how the 2-tick hold gets judged. Debug overlay, per [Debug Visualisation](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md#debug-visualisation)
- **The sandbox's at-rest reset gives way to the player**, per [Roadmap](../roadmap.md#notes-on-the-order)'s *Scenario reset*: it stays for ball-only physics scenarios, and nothing else. With a player in the scenario, reset on out, goal, or the player leaving the frame, and put the player back with the ball
- **Player state is data, kept apart from the node that draws it**, per *Simulation state* in [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md) — the first state beside the ball's. The carry's feel values go in their own tuning Resource, per [Live Tuning](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md#live-tuning-while-running)
- **The first kitted sprite, so recolouring lands here** — one kit and one complexion for the lone player, per [Recolouring](../../docs/design/match-engine/kits.md#recolouring). Two kits and clashes wait for a second team
- **The first milestone session, so the playtest log starts here.** Its shape and location are decided when the file is made, per [Session Habits](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md#session-habits)
- **Filled in before [0.4 Deck Build](0.4-deck-build.md) was Done, by choice.** Project → *Status* asks for 0.4 first; if its Deck run turns anything up that reaches this milestone, re-check this file against it

**Attributes:** Speed sets top running speed, and how fast the run cycle turns over. Ball Control sets how fast the ball creeps out of the carrier's reach, and how many direction changes it takes to lose touch under pressure — counted here, costing nothing until Phase 2. Values in [On-the-Ball Mechanics](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md).

**Sprites:** the outfield player standing and running in every facing — the first sprite with a body, and the first with kit and skin entries — at the cell, anchor and frame timing in [Movement](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#movement). Sideloaded per [Sideloaded Art](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#sideloaded-art), placeholders otherwise.

**Testing:** no sideloaded data, per [Local Data](../../docs/decisions/licensing-and-ip.md#local-data). Which device produced an input, and the deadzone, are the Input Map's — engine behaviour and configuration, per [What a Test Has to Earn](../../ways-of-working/automation-testing.md#what-a-test-has-to-earn).

- The stick snaps to the nearest eighth on each side of every boundary, per [Input as Intent](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#input-as-intent) — clear of exact ties, which are its Open Question
- Each of [Movement](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#movement)'s criteria that 1.1 reaches — facing and moving on the tick the stick changes and kept while it's centred; running speed at every Speed, measured from the player's motion, 127/128 along +x and +y; the run frame's time at every Speed, running and carrying, and its restart. Off-the-stick facing is [2.1](2.1-keeper-drill.md)'s
- Each of [Carrying the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/on-the-ball-mechanics.md#carrying-the-ball)'s criteria — the pick-up at each edge of its distance and height, and with the stick centred; carrying speed at every Speed; the creep within its 10% at every Ball Control; a turn out of reach leaving the ball; the catch-up boost at its angle. The pressure rule against an opponent set up in the test, since the rule is built here: touch lost on exactly the table's held change at every Ball Control, never unopposed, and the count reset by centring the stick. Opponents who are down wait for [1.3](1.3-contextual-action.md)
- The player's frame is a function of their state alone, per [Sheets](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#sheets)
- Every kit colour lands in its region's reserved entry, and a complexion in the skin and hair entries, with no art touched — per [Recolouring](../../docs/design/match-engine/kits.md#recolouring), every region and colour
- Determinism — the same scenario and the same scripted intent run twice end in the same state, per [Automation Testing](../../ways-of-working/automation-testing.md#determinism)
- The shared per-tick invariant check gains the player's: no NaN position, facing always one of eight

**360 Testing:** the first sprite besides the ball, so [Showing Height](../../docs/design/match-engine/ball-physics-and-aerial-simulation.md#showing-height)'s layering — tested at [0.3](0.3-ball-physics-test-suite.md) on the ball and its shadow alone — can be asserted against a body: a ball at any height layers behind a player standing lower on screen, and the shadow under a player beside it. 0.3's reset-at-rest test assumed no player, so it holds for a ball-only scenario and needs saying so. Nothing for [0.1](0.1-input-map.md) or [0.4](0.4-deck-build.md), which have no tests to widen.
