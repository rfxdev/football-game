# 3.3 Decision Loop

**Status:** Outline

Replace the random goalward kick with something coherent: in range and facing goal → shoot, else react to the nearest opponent — dribble, pass to an open teammate, or sidestep — per [The CPU on the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#the-cpu-on-the-ball). Off the ball, the same loop decides when a player presses the action button — a slide below chest height, a header above it, per [1.3 Contextual Action](1.3-contextual-action.md). The acts themselves, and how they're contested, are 1.3's and [2.3 Contests](2.3-contests.md)'s. Crude but legible.

**Asking:** Are the AI's choices *legible*? Watching a passage of play, can you say why each player did what they did?

**Session shape:** full scenarios, plus reviewing recordings — legibility is much easier to judge on replay than in the moment

**Notes:**

- **Log inexplicable decisions, not suboptimal ones** — coherent beats clever. The overlay naming the rule that fired is what makes a decision explicable on replay, per [The CPU on the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#the-cpu-on-the-ball)
- **Track whether a tackle felt *cheap*.** An AI that dispossesses you with a lunge you couldn't have countered isn't a bug, but it will make the game unfun long before any bug does. The CPU's slides come from its second player near the ball, never from behind, per [The CPU's Tackle](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#the-cpus-tackle)
- **Watch for CPU headers that can't connect.** The reference's CPU heads on ball height and distance alone, never checking the ball will come within reach, so it sometimes jumps at nothing — per [The CPU's Controlled Player](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#the-cpus-controlled-player). Clone it as is and judge whether a whiff reads as a fair miss or as stupidity. If stupidity, the fix is a departure, after the clone plays: head only when the ball's flight brings it into the connect band during the jump — cheap, since the flight is deterministic
- **Only the two players going for the ball leave shape.** Shape from [3.2](3.2-shape-and-home-zones.md) plus that one rule is most of what makes play look intentional — see [Who Goes for the Ball](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#who-goes-for-the-ball)

**Testing:** the AI decision as a pure function — a snapshot of the match and the seed in, an intent out, per [Decision Rate](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#decision-rate) — makes it table-testable: ball there, opponent here, expect this, with no scene tree and no timing involved.
