# 3.3 Decision Loop

**Status:** Outline

Replace the random goalward kick with something coherent: open teammate → pass, else in range and angle → shoot, else dribble goalward. Off the ball, the same loop decides when a player presses the action button — a slide below chest height, a header above it, per [1.3 Contextual Action](1.3-contextual-action.md). The acts themselves, and how they're contested, are 1.3's and [2.3 Contests](2.3-contests.md)'s. Crude but legible.

**Asking:** Are the AI's choices *legible*? Watching a passage of play, can you say why each player did what they did?

**Session shape:** full scenarios, plus reviewing recordings — legibility is much easier to judge on replay than in the moment

**Notes:**

- **Log inexplicable decisions, not suboptimal ones** — coherent beats clever, per [Decisions](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#decisions)
- **Track whether a tackle felt *cheap*.** An AI that dispossesses you with a lunge you couldn't have countered isn't a bug, but it will make the game unfun long before any bug does. The reference's CPU presses the button on two triggers, per *AI Behavior* in [Match Mechanics](../../sources/swos/match-mechanics.md): a tackle that ignores ball height, and a header or volley at goal timed on it. No defensive heading has been found yet — worth searching for before the design takes it on, since the button's meaning comes from the height whatever the CPU intended
- **Watch for CPU headers that can't connect.** The reference's CPU heads on ball height and distance alone, never checking the ball will come within reach, so it sometimes jumps at nothing. Clone it as is and judge whether a whiff reads as a fair miss or as stupidity. If stupidity, the fix is a departure: head only when the ball's flight brings it into the connect band during the jump — cheap, since the flight is deterministic
- **The nearest-player press rule is the reference's floor, worth matching before anything cleverer.** Shape from [3.2](3.2-shape-and-home-zones.md) plus that one rule is most of what makes play look intentional — see [Roles](../../docs/design/match-engine/team-ai-and-decision-making.md#roles)

**Testing:** the AI decision as a pure function — a perception snapshot in, an intent out — makes it table-testable: ball there, opponent here, expect this, with no scene tree and no timing involved.
