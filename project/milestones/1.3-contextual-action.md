# 1.3 Contextual Action

**Status:** Outline

The action button branches on the ball's height: below chest height it slides, above it heads. Both actions exist here; contesting them with anyone waits for Phase 2.

**Notes:**

- **One milestone, not two.** Slide and header share the button, and the ball's height at the moment of the press decides which fires. They are one branch — neither can be built without deciding the other, and splitting them would mean building the branch twice
- **The slide is a fixed burst, independent of the player's own Speed rating** — a hardcoded lunge that decays at a flat rate over about 19 frames, the same for every player and faster than any player can run. It is a committed action, not a fast run, and it should feel like one
- **Tap or hold sets the slide's strength, and strength shows twice** — in how hard the ball comes off the connect and in how long the slider lies down after. Releasing within the slide's first two frames makes a weak tackle; holding makes a strong one, and the CPU always tackles strong. Both are pure input, so both are here rather than waiting for an opponent — numbers in *Sliding*, [Match Mechanics](../../sources/swos/match-mechanics.md)
- **Heading splits into dive and jump by stick position at the press**, and they take their aim differently — the dive aims during the dive within a ±45° window, the jump flicks on freely once airborne. Worth confirming both exist before judging either
- Fouls are not here. A slide that misses the ball and hits a player is [2.3 Tackling](2.3-tackling.md); with nobody to hit, this milestone covers the lunge, the connect and the recovery

**Attributes:** Heading sets aerial reach and how accurately a header is redirected. The lunge reads none — it is the same for every player, per the note above. Tackling sets how long the slider lies down after a strong tackle: 30 frames at Tackling 0 down to 9 at Tackling 7. A weak tackle ignores it and recovers in a flat 3 frames.

**Sprites:** the slide and the lying-down recovery after it, and both headers. The slide's frames fit its 19-frame lunge rather than setting it. The jump is the first time a player leaves the ground, so it takes the shadow and sort-order conventions the ball already uses, per [Ball Physics](../../docs/design/match-engine/ball-physics-and-aerial-simulation.md#showing-height).
