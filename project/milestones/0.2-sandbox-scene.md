# 0.2 Sandbox Scene

**Status:** Ready

A fixed frame the size of the Deck's screen — 426 × 266 world units at a scale of 3, per [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md) — with a ball that moves, has height, rolls, and casts a shadow, built to [Ball Physics and Aerial Simulation](../../docs/design/match-engine/ball-physics-and-aerial-simulation.md). Boundary and goal detection, with **scenario reset** as their only consequence. No camera, no player, no pitch markings, no rules.

**Notes:**

- **The frame is fixed — no camera, no scrolling, nothing following the ball.** The sandbox *is* the screen: the ball only ever leaves the frame by crossing a boundary, which resets it. A non-16:10 display shows empty world around the frame rather than bars — `expand` adds none — which is fine here, because nothing in this scene is player-facing
- **Check that a moving sprite stays sharp**, per [Pixels](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#pixels), since the ball is the first thing that moves. `canvas_items` stretch at a whole-number scale should keep it crisp, per [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md). If it doesn't, `viewport` stretch is the fallback: it draws at 426 × 266 and scales the whole frame, always sharp but moving in whole world pixels — 3 screen pixels at a time on the Deck
- **The ball gets a height here, but no aerial mechanics.** The fake-Z *foundation* is groundwork because the shadow and height-gated collision are part of ball feel from the first moment — a ball that bounces without a shadow reads as broken. Lofted kicking and heading are *mechanics* built on top of it and arrive in Phase 1. See [Ball Physics and Aerial Simulation](../../docs/design/match-engine/ball-physics-and-aerial-simulation.md)
- **Boundaries reset the scenario.** A boundary predicate plus a reset costs almost nothing, and the predicate is the same one [5.2 Out of Play](5.2-out-of-play.md) needs, so none of it is throwaway
- **The frame's edges are constants**, read by the out rule in [The Pitch](../../docs/design/match-engine/pitch-and-environment.md#the-pitch), never the viewport's size — so [3.0 Pitch and Camera](3.0-pitch-and-camera.md) moves the constants to the pitch's lines rather than rewriting the predicate
- **The goal is a predicate, not a thing you can see.** Line crossed **and** under the bar, per [The Goal](../../docs/design/match-engine/pitch-and-environment.md#the-goal), over a configurable line segment — no posts, no net, no score. It lands here because it is the second consumer of the ball's height, so it and the shadow get debugged together rather than the goal's height term getting its first exercise inside a shooting drill. Something to actually shoot at arrives with [2.1 Keeper Drill](2.1-keeper-drill.md)
- **The scenario is set in the Inspector** — the ball's start, the point it heads for, its speed and its vertical speed, exported on the scene. It resets on a boundary, a goal, or the ball coming to rest. No input action, so nothing is spent from the controls table, per [the gate](../../ways-of-working/spec-chain.md#the-gate)
- **Scenario reset is the drill harness.** It carries all the way to Phase 4: every Phase 2 drill needs resetting anyway, and it is what lets the entire restart system wait until the formation system can answer where everyone stands
- **The first simulation code, so Determinism starts here** — the ball updates in `_physics_process`, against the injected tick clock, per [Architecture and Stack](../../docs/decisions/architecture-and-stack.md)
- **The ball's values go in its own tuning Resource**, the first one, per [Live Tuning](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md#live-tuning-while-running)
- **The sprite pipeline lands with the first sprites** — sheets and their descriptions, the palette shader, the match picking the frame, and the sideload reader for sheets and the match palette — per [Sheets](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#sheets) and [Sideloaded Art](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#sideloaded-art), with placeholders for both. The ball has no kit, so the shader's first palette is the fixed entries alone
- **The sandbox stays alive after this milestone** rather than being deleted — every later system gets tuned in it too, per [Playtesting](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md)
- **No Asking, Session shape or Attributes.** Every ball criterion fixes a value, so this exits on the check, sharpness judged by eye — per [Exit Criteria](../README.md#exit-criteria) — and there's no player to read an attribute
- **The ball's own tests are [0.3 Ball Physics Test Suite](0.3-ball-physics-test-suite.md)'s deliverable**, so Testing here covers only the sheets and the sideload reader. No 360 Testing: [0.1 Input Map](0.1-input-map.md) is configuration with no tests, and nothing here reads input

**Sprites:** the ball — still, and rolling through four frames — and its shadow, at the size and anchor in [Showing Height](../../docs/design/match-engine/ball-physics-and-aerial-simulation.md#showing-height), each a sheet per [Sheets](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#sheets). Sideloaded per [Sideloaded Art](../../docs/design/match-engine/sprites.md#sideloaded-art), placeholders otherwise.

**Testing:** every cell of a sheet draws with its anchor at the sprite's position. A sideloaded sheet draws at exactly its placeholder's position, size and layer; a sheet of the wrong size, or not 8-bit greyscale, draws the placeholder instead; a palette of the wrong size falls back to the project's. Synthetic fixtures only, per [Local Data](../../docs/decisions/licensing-and-ip.md#local-data).
