# 0.2 Sandbox Scene

**Status:** Outline

A fixed 1280×800 frame with a ball that moves, has height, and casts a shadow. Boundary and goal detection, with **scenario reset** as their only consequence. No camera, no player, no pitch markings, no rules.

**Notes:**

- **The frame is fixed — no camera, no scrolling, nothing following the ball.** The sandbox *is* the screen: the ball only ever leaves the frame by crossing a boundary, which resets it. Bars on a non-16:10 display are fine here, because nothing in this scene is player-facing
- **The ball gets a height here, but no aerial mechanics.** The fake-Z *foundation* is groundwork because the shadow and height-gated collision are part of ball feel from the first moment — a ball that bounces without a shadow reads as broken. Lofted kicking and heading are *mechanics* built on top of it and arrive in Phase 1. See [Ball Physics and Aerial Simulation](../../docs/design/match-engine/ball-physics-and-aerial-simulation.md)
- **Boundaries reset the scenario.** A boundary predicate plus a reset costs almost nothing, and the predicate is the same one [5.2 Out of Play](5.2-out-of-play.md) needs, so none of it is throwaway
- **The goal is a predicate, not a thing you can see.** Line crossed **and** under the bar, over a configurable line segment — no posts, no net, no score. It lands here because it is the second consumer of the ball's height, so it and the shadow get debugged together rather than the goal's height term getting its first exercise inside a shooting drill. Something to actually shoot at arrives with [2.1 Keeper Drill](2.1-keeper-drill.md)
- **Scenario reset is the drill harness.** It carries all the way to Phase 4: every Phase 2 drill needs resetting anyway, and it is what lets the entire restart system wait until the formation system can answer where everyone stands
- **The sandbox stays alive after this milestone** rather than being deleted — every later system gets tuned in it too, per [Playtesting](../../ways-of-working/playtesting.md)

**Sprites:** the ball and its shadow.
